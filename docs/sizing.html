Sizing

The server, disk, and memory a filesystem of a billion files needs, and how many people one process serves.

ItemRule of thumb
fs-inventory scan outputabout 40 bytes per entry (40 GB per billion), for each scan kept; Pinax never deletes them (Scans)
Snapshot indexabout 56 bytes per file (56 GB per billion), times PINAX_KEEP_SNAPSHOTS (6), plus about 60 bytes per file new or removed since the scan before
Building an indexfree space for the new index beside the kept ones (it replaces the oldest only after the ingest), and while it builds, for a staged copy of the files and DuckDB's spill files: about 2–3 times an index in all
Index buildabout 1.2 seconds per million files on 10 cores (100 million in two minutes, a billion in about 20 minutes, measured on synthetic data); 8 GB of memory is enough, spilling beside the index beyond it, and more is no faster
Query memoryPINAX_QUERY_MEMORY_LIMIT (8 GB) for every open index together, shared equally among up to 8, in the one web process: what their queries use at once and the blocks they keep. More memory left over for the operating system's page cache keeps indexes fast
Long requestsPinax stops a request after PINAX_QUERY_TIMEOUT (180 seconds), or when the browser leaves; the proxy in front must wait longer (oauth2-proxy's upstream_timeout is 5 minutes in deploy/oauth2-proxy/pinax.cfg, against its own default of 30 seconds; nginx's proxy_read_timeout is 60 seconds by default). PINAX_WIDE_QUERIES (2) searches of a whole filesystem run at once, each on every core (Many people at once)
Catalogsmall: about one row per top-level folder per scan for history (2,000 folders scanned weekly add about 100,000 rows a year)

Put /var/lib/pinax/snapshots on fast local SSD or NVMe: indexes are read with many small range requests. Scans are read in one pass, at ingest and rebuild, so they can live on slower, cheaper disk: point PINAX_SCAN_ROOT there, and add it to the units' ReadWritePaths. For a billion-file filesystem with the defaults, six indexes take about 340 GB and six scans about 240 GB, plus any older scans not yet deleted.

Many people at once

One web process answers every request. Measured with the load test (Many people at once) on a laptop (Apple M4: 4 performance and 6 efficiency cores, 32 GB), against three scans of 10 million files, with each person opening a page every 5 to 15 seconds:

One web process, on the laptop
People at once, every page within its budget100 on SQLite or PostgreSQL, at a median of 40 to 60 ms and a 95th percentile of 110 to 180 ms; about one core busy
Before pages slow downabout 25 to 28 pages a second: some 250 to 300 people at that pace. Past it nothing fails, but pages wait their turn: at four times the pace, a median page of about a second
What bounds itPython's work for each page (the catalog, the answer), which runs one thread at a time: two or three cores were busy at the most. Faster cores serve more pages; more cores do not, though they speed whole-filesystem searches and index builds
Searches of whole filesystemseach uses every core while it runs, two at a time (PINAX_WIDE_QUERIES). With eight administrators searching one search after another among 100 people, others' pages stayed within their budgets on SQLite, 95% of them within half a second
Sign-ins200 people signing in at the same moment are all signed in within 6 to 9 seconds, but their first pages then take up to a minute, while the process works through them; people arriving over a few minutes do not wait
Memory1.2 to 3 GB for the web app; queries may use up to PINAX_QUERY_MEMORY_LIMIT (8 GB) in all
CatalogSQLite serves this as well as PostgreSQL; PostgreSQL adds a round trip to each catalog question (1.5 ms here, through a virtual machine; less on a server's network)

A site's people are rarely all clicking at once: a few hundred at a time is a large organization's busy hour. For more:

WhenChange
Pages slow at busy times, with only two or three cores busyA server with faster cores. PINAX_WEB_THREADS=8 served a third more pages a second when every page was quick, but then a few whole-filesystem searches waiting for their turn hold most of the threads: keep 40 where those are common
Pages slow while whole-filesystem searches runFewer at once (PINAX_WIDE_QUERIES, 2) leave other pages more of the cores, and the searches wait longer for a turn. PINAX_QUERY_THREADS below the cores slowed the searches and gave other pages little back in the load test, since a search and the pages of the same index share its threads
People see "Pinax is busy"PostgreSQL: raise PINAX_DATABASE_POOL_SIZE within the database's max_connections
More people than one process servesNot yet: more than one web process needs the turns, metrics, and searches in the background shared between them

A filesystem of two billion files

Projected from the 100-million-file measurements (Measured performance), with the defaults:

Two billion files
Each scanabout 80 GB; the six of the kept snapshots about 480 GB, on cheaper disk
Each indexabout 112 GB, so about 670 GB for six, plus file_changes (about 2.4 GB for a week in which 2% of the files are new or removed)
Building an indexabout 45 minutes on 10 cores, with 8 GB of memory, and 250–350 GB of free space while it runs: about 1–1.2 TB of NVMe for the indexes in all
Web server memoryPINAX_QUERY_MEMORY_LIMIT for queries in every open index together (8 GB by default; raise it with the server's memory), plus page cache for the indexes: 64 GB or more
Age map, types, owners, cold folders, history, chargestens of milliseconds, as at 100 million: they read folder totals, not files
A lab's searchgrows with the lab's files: a lab of 300 million files searches like a filesystem of that size, in several seconds
A search of the whole filesystemabout a minute, then about as long again for the count of matches; two at a time
What changed since the previous scanthe new and removed files read file_changes, fast; the folder lists read every folder, about 0.1 s per 250,000 folders, so tens of seconds at 50 million folders or more
What changed of the whole filesystem since an older scancompares both scans' files, minutes: longer than the time limit, so ask it of a folder
Rebuilding six indexes after an upgrade that changes the index formatabout 5 hours, one after another; the old indexes answer meanwhile

These are projections from synthetic scans with about 41 files per folder. Production filesystems often have fewer files per folder, and everything that reads every folder (the build's subtree totals, cold folders, and comparison, and whole-filesystem folder reports) grows with folders, so measure the first production scan: Scans gives its build time and index size, its manifest the count of folders, and the web app's journal names each request Pinax stopped (Stopped /api/files: timeout after 180 s).
