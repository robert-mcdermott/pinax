The demo

Pinax with fictional labs and six months of scans, in one command, and a walk-through with a step for each role.

The demo shows Pinax with no setup: two research filesystems, six months of scans, labs and departments, people to sign in as, and storage prices. Everything it shows comes from real fs-inventory scans of files the seed writes. This guide says how to run it, what is in it, and how to walk through it.

Running it

Requires uv. Install the pinned fs-inventory release into bin/ (the script verifies its SHA-256 checksum), then the Python packages, then the demo:

scripts/install-fs-inventory.sh
uv sync
uv run pinax seed
uv run pinax serve

Then open http://127.0.0.1:8001 and choose who to be.

pinax seed writes two demo filesystems of about 35,000 sparse files (123 TB of logical size on under 10 MB of disk), with realistic ages going back fifteen years. It scans each one six times with fs-inventory, with six months of simulated activity in between, dates the scans a month apart (the last one today), and ingests them. It takes about fifteen seconds. Run it again at any time to start over: it resets var/ and demo/ each time, so it also undoes whatever you changed during a demo. --scale 0.5 writes about half as many files (the walk-through's numbers are for the default).

The demo's catalog is SQLite in var/, with nothing else to run. It runs the same on PostgreSQL: with the driver installed (uv sync --extra postgres) and PINAX_DATABASE_URL pointing at an empty database, pinax seed fills it and pinax serve uses it (PostgreSQL).

Sign-in in the demo is a persona switcher (PINAX_AUTH_MODE=demo, the default in the demo): anyone can be anyone, and Switch identity (top right) changes who you are. That is fine on a laptop and wrong anywhere else, so Pinax refuses to start in demo mode when PINAX_ENVIRONMENT=prod. And since seeding deletes the catalog, pinax seed runs only when PINAX_ENVIRONMENT=demo (the default), never on a catalog set up for real data. To try Pinax on real data instead, see SETUP.md.

The six months

The six scans of each filesystem are dated a month apart, the last one on the day you seed. Every month the Genomics Shared Resource delivers two Illumina runs and a nanopore run, the Fujimoto Lab adds a batch of samples (it grows from 21 to 27 TB), and a few other labs add results. Besides that:

ScanSince the scan before
1The starting point, five months ago
2The Fujimoto Lab's Cell Ranger results arrive; the Chen and Haddad labs delete temporary files and checkpoints
3A light-sheet project moves from the Bennett Lab to the Haddad Lab (haddad_n/datasets/2024_lightsheet_bennett)
4The Quintero Lab joins, mostly with data from the PI's previous institution: new here, but years old
5The Lindqvist Lab compresses its uncompressed FASTQ files (wgs/2024_germline/fastq_uncompressed becomes fastq)
6The Fujimoto Lab writes its atlas; the Dasgupta Lab deletes its 2016 cohort, as the cleanup plan it made after scan 5 says, and renames the manuscript folder it planned to delete (manuscripts/2021_draft becomes 2021_published)

Meanwhile nobody touches the Kowalski Lab's raw/2024_tmt_plex, which ages past two years: the lab's cold share climbs from 64% to 100% at the same size. History shows all of it, the Change map where each change is, and What changed lists them.

Prices

Storage has prices, so the six months make six months of charges. Fast costs $25 per TB a month, and $22 from the 16th of last month; every lab has a terabyte of it free and departments none, and the Lindqvist Lab has its own 5 TB from the 10th of last month (a grant's allocation). Secure costs $40 per TiB, with nothing free.

Capacity

Fast holds 120 TiB, so at its trend it fills within the year, and an expansion to 250 TiB is scheduled for eleven months after the seed, too late. Secure holds a tebibyte. The demo's scans record nothing about the storage (its files are sparse, on a laptop's disk), so the capacities are set, and use is measured by the files' size.

The walk-through (about 20 minutes)

  1. Home page. Choose Continue as Hanna Lindqvist (a PI). My data shows one folder: 19 TiB, most of it unchanged for years.

  2. Age map. Open it. wgs/2019_tumor_normal is a large blue tile: 17 TiB of alignments last changed about seven years ago. Hover for details; switch Color by to Time since last access and Size by to Size of sequencing and genomics.

  3. File types. The callout shows terabytes of uncompressed SAM files; click .sam to list them. (The lab compressed its uncompressed FASTQ files before the fifth scan.)

  4. Switch identity (top right) to Aiko Fujimoto. Drill into projects: 2025_immune_aging is red, data written this year, beside two blue projects. Then 2022_tumor_atlas, then raw: an active project hides 2014_legacy_reruns, the darkest blue tile, nearly twelve years old. Go back to fujimoto_a and switch View to Icicle: the same folder shows at once, four levels down; Sunburst draws it as rings. Cold data at two years lists raw as one cold unit; at ten years it lists 2014_legacy_reruns alone. History for fujimoto_a: the lab grew from 19 to 25 TiB in six scans, and new data on top of the columns brought its cold share down from 77% to 60%. Hover a column for that scan; the three small charts follow. The dashed line is the forecast: at its trend, about 34 TiB in a year (In a year), within the shaded range. The bennett_k lab has none: the project it handed to haddad_n is a step, and the trend starts again after it, so it needs six scans after the step.

  5. Switch to Jordan Okafor, a delegate for two labs. My data shows both labs' folders; the age map puts both side by side. Then switch to Valeria Quintero, whose lab joined three scans ago: one folder, two thirds of it unchanged for more than two years, because most of it came from the PI's previous institution. Its History starts at the fourth scan.

  6. Storage charges. Switch back to Hanna Lindqvist. My data shows this month's storage charge, an estimate to the month's end. Open Storage charges and choose last month: the price of Fast fell on the 16th, and the lab's own 5 TB free tier started on the 10th, so the month is worked out in three parts, each its average size less what was free, at its price, for its days. Past months lists the months since the first scan, and The next 12 months what the lab will pay if it keeps growing as it has, with a range.

  7. Cleanup plans. Switch to Rohan Dasgupta. Cleanup plans shows the plan his lab made after the fifth scan. The sixth shows 2016_cohort_A done, under Done so far with what it frees and saves a month; 2021_draft no longer at its path, but a folder with its inode is at 2021_published: it was renamed, so nothing was freed (It moved there makes the plan follow it); and 2018_cohort_B still to do, with what it would save. Suggestions lists folders worth adding. Open the age map at a folder of the lab: its panel's Cleanup plan box adds it to the plan.

  8. Tags. Switch to Aiko Fujimoto. On the age map of fujimoto_a/projects, the tiles with a dot are tagged: 2022_tumor_atlas has a grant, a project, a source, a stage, and the lab's own Atlas paper, which its panel lists, and its subfolders inherit; 2018_pilot is kept until 2030, so its Cleanup plan box says it cannot go in a plan. Storage charges splits the lab's month by grant: the tumor atlas's grant, the immune aging study's, and what is not tagged. Collections with Source is sequencing and Data stage is raw finds the Genomics Shared Resource's runs and the Lindqvist Lab's tumor-normal study (its source inherited from wgs).

  9. Saved views. Still as Aiko Fujimoto, My data lists Tumor atlas, oldest data, a view she saved and shared with her lab: it opens the age map of the tumor atlas colored by its oldest files. Save view on any report keeps another.

  10. Switch to Priya Natarajan (storage administrator). All data shows both filesystems, the largest folders, the biggest changes since the previous scan (a batch of samples and an atlas in fujimoto_a, sequencing runs in gsr_illumina), and three unmapped folders. Open the age map of Fast: scratch_legacy is a large, very old, unmapped tile. Open History for kowalski_d: the same size every scan, while its cold share climbs to 100%. Then lindqvist_h/wgs/2024_germline: a terabyte smaller at the fifth scan, when the lab compressed its FASTQ files. Open the Change map of Fast and compare with the first scan, five months ago: fujimoto_a and haddad_n grew, bennett_k shrank by 81%. In bennett_k, the removed imaging/2024_lightsheet keeps the room it had; in haddad_n, datasets/2024_lightsheet_bennett is new, and its tooltip says it is over a year old: it moved. What changed for Fast since the first scan lists the Fujimoto project and the sequencing runs where it grew, the moved project and the compressed FASTQ among new and removed folders, and the largest new files (Cell Ranger BAMs); each list downloads as CSV.

  11. Folders and labs. Tick Unmapped only, then map shared_reference to the Genomics Shared Resource. Switch to Casey Morgan: the folder now appears in their data. Back as Priya, Tags lists the Fujimoto Lab's own Atlas paper under Labs' own tags: Fold into a standard tag into Project gives its folder a Project tag named Atlas paper, and retires the lab's tag.

  12. Costs and Chargeback. Back as Priya, Costs shows each filesystem's prices and free tiers, with the Lindqvist Lab's exception. Chargeback for last month lists every lab's and department's charge, the most first (the Genomics Shared Resource, a department, has nothing free), the totals by filesystem, and the storage no one owns, most of it scratch_legacy; Download CSV is the statement for finance. This month, shared_reference is charged to the Genomics Shared Resource from the moment it was mapped. The next 12 months adds up what every lab will pay at its trend. Capacity: Fast is 92% used and fills in about eight months at its trend, a few months before the expansion scheduled for eleven months on, so All data flags it under Needs attention. Its History draws the capacity above the columns.

  13. Storage health. An unreadable folder, a very large directory (12,000 PNG tiles), problem file names (a newline, a leading dash, a Windows-reserved character, a name that is not NFC), and files owned by accounts that no longer exist.

  14. Find files. Search *.bam, older than five years, largest first; download the CSV. On all of Fast, Run in the background runs the search past a page's time limit: Searches already has one, every BAM on Fast, done by the seed. And the age map of lindqvist_h/wgs/2019_tumor_normal/from_core shows four hard links to the core's FASTQ files, counted once, at the core.

  15. Scans. Six snapshots of each filesystem, a month apart, each with its full index (PINAX_KEEP_SNAPSHOTS keeps six), and whether automatic ingest is watching for new scans. Click a date to open that scan: the header says it is an older one, every report and link stays on it, and Back to the latest returns. Choose another scan beside the date on any report.

  16. People and access. Add person: a username, a name, an email, and a lab; the panel shows the folders they will see. Grant or remove the storage administrator role. Deactivate them, and they cannot sign in (their access is kept, unused); Reactivate restores it. Each change is in the person's record and the audit log. On the Labs and departments tab, each lab's folders and members; Add lab or department adds one (a slug, a name, and whether it is a lab or a department, which decides its free tier), and its page changes its details or deactivates it: its members stop seeing its folders, which stay its own (and charged to it) until they are moved.

  17. Site roles. Site roles on the People tab lists the three people with one, each given on their page and in the audit log. Switch to each in turn:

    • Helen Marsh, an auditor, sees every page an administrator does, from All data and Folders and labs to People and access and the audit log, and no button that changes anything. The server refuses her changes too.
    • Daniel Osei, in research finance, lands on Chargeback. His sidebar is Storage charges (of any lab or department), Chargeback with its statement, Costs, and Capacity, to read; he sees no folders. API tokens, under his name, has his Monthly ledger import, which reads the statement every month.
    • Grace Whitfield, head of Basic Sciences, sees the division's labs as their members do, to read. My data is the Alvarez, Fujimoto, and Kowalski labs, 28.5 TiB, and Storage charges and Cleanup plans choose among them. On the age map of fujimoto_a/projects/2022_tumor_atlas, the panel lists the folder's tags and whether it is in the lab's plan, with no button to change either: tagging and planning stay with each lab's PI and delegates.

  18. API tokens. As Priya Natarajan, API tokens in the sidebar: her own token, the service tokens for the storage dashboards and the finance warehouse, and Daniel Osei's, Rohan Dasgupta's, and Mateo Reyes's, with when each was last used. Make token, reading only, shows it once; then, in a terminal:

    curl -H "Authorization: Bearer THE-TOKEN" "http://127.0.0.1:8001/script/api/admin/costs/charges?fmt=csv"

    This month's chargeback statement, as Priya sees it. Revoke it, and the same request answers 401.

  19. The audit log. As Helen Marsh, the auditor, Audit log: every change above, newest first, with who made it, from where, and with which token. Choose Rohan Dasgupta for his cleanup plan, Change: All: API tokens for every token made and revoked, or the filesystem Fast and the folder dasgupta_r for everything done to that lab's folder. Download CSV has them all.

  20. Access review. Still as Helen, Access review: two things worth a look (Riya Patel, who can sign in and sees nothing, and Rohan Dasgupta's Sequencing pipeline tags, a token that can make changes as Rohan, such as tagging the lab's folders), every site role, each lab's members and folders, and each person's access, with when each was given. Type Basic Sciences in Find for that division: its three labs and Grace Whitfield. Helen's review is under Reviews; Record a review adds another, and it is in the audit log.

  21. Import from CSV. In a terminal, uv run pinax import examples/import lists the 14 changes the example files would make: two departments, a lab, three people, their memberships, and owners for the three unmapped folders. Add --apply, and Folders and labs shows no unmapped folders; every change is in the audit log.

  22. Logs and metrics. The terminal running pinax serve has a line for each request, with who made it, from where, how long it took, and its request ID; each change made above is there too, as a pinax.audit line. Stop the server, and start it again with metrics on:

    PINAX_METRICS_TOKEN=demo-metrics-token-0123456789 uv run pinax serve
    curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer demo-metrics-token-0123456789" http://127.0.0.1:8001/metrics | grep '^pinax_filesystem'

    Each filesystem's size, files, cold data, capacity, use, and growth, and the day Fast could fill at the fast end of its trend. uv run pinax db status says the catalog is up to date.

Afterwards

Changes made during the walk-through (a folder mapped, a person or lab added, an import applied) stay until you seed again:

uv run pinax seed
